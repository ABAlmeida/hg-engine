# Challenge Capture Rules Plan

Last updated: 2026-08-04

This document records the reviewed design for the challenge capture rules.
The save-data foundation, ordinary encounter rules, Safari behavior, and
Contest retention rules are implemented. The earlier Contest flow was
manually verified; Safari and the revised retention-on-success condition still
require focused manual verification.

## Decisions

- The rules are active immediately on a new save. They are not gated on
  receiving Poké Balls.
- Each displayed map section permits one eligible, non-duplicate encounter.
- Duplicate checks use the existing Pokédex caught state and compare the exact
  species only. For example, catching Zubat does not make Golbat a duplicate.
- Any source that marks a species as caught in the Pokédex can make that exact
  species a duplicate later, including gifts and static encounters.
- The shiny clause overrides consumed-area restrictions, but not the
  exact-species duplicate restriction. A genuinely shiny, non-duplicate
  Pokémon may be caught without consuming the area's normal opportunity.
- Pokémon generated by Shiny Bait are genuinely shiny and qualify for the
  shiny clause. This deliberately makes Shiny Bait a special capture
  opportunity rather than merely a visual variant of Poké Bait.
- Encounter areas use `BATTLE_PARAM.map_section`. No area override table is
  planned.
- Wild double battles remain disabled.
- Safari Zone and Bug-Catching Contest encounters use the special rules below.
- Existing-save migration is not required.

## Ordinary eligible encounters

Eligible encounter sources are:

- Poké Bait and Shiny Bait
- fishing
- Headbutt
- Rock Smash
- Sweet Scent and Honey
- surfing
- passive wild encounters, if they are restored later

When an eligible encounter starts:

1. Check the encountered Pokémon's actual shiny state and whether its exact
   species is already marked caught in the Pokédex.
2. If it is shiny and not a duplicate, allow capture and do not read or update
   `consumedAreas` for that encounter.
3. If it is a duplicate, show the duplicate restriction, do not consume the
   area, and prevent capture even when the area was already consumed.
4. Read its displayed map section from `BATTLE_PARAM.map_section`.
5. If that map section is already present in `consumedAreas`, prevent capture.
6. Otherwise, mark the map section consumed immediately and allow capture
   attempts for that encounter.

Static and scripted encounters, roamers, gifts, eggs, trades, the catching
tutorial, Pal Park, wireless battles, and debug battles do not consume an
ordinary encounter area. Their normal Pokédex updates are unchanged and can
still affect later duplicate checks.

## Area save data

Use a `consumedAreas` bitset keyed by the same map-section identifier domain as
`BATTLE_PARAM.map_section`. Size it from the relevant named map-section count or
maximum rather than a raw numeric constant.

This model deliberately stores displayed areas, not individual maps. Maps that
share a displayed map section therefore share one capture opportunity.

No area override table is needed for the current rules.

## Safari Zone

The Safari Zone can use the same consumed-area bitset as ordinary encounters.
The map data was checked: `MAP_SAFARI_ZONE_01` through
`MAP_SAFARI_ZONE_14`, plus the encounter-enabled entrance exterior, all use
the same displayed Safari Zone map section. One saved bit therefore represents
the entire Safari Zone, including its different internal areas.

Safari behavior:

1. If the encountered Pokémon is shiny and not a duplicate, allow Safari Ball
   use regardless of the saved Safari state and do not consume the standard
   Safari opportunity.
2. On the first Safari encounter without that exemption, immediately set the
   bit for `BATTLE_PARAM.map_section`.
3. Apply the normal exact-species Pokédex duplicate check.
4. If a non-exempt Pokémon is already caught, show the duplicate restriction
   and block Safari Ball use. Consume the Safari opportunity if it was unused;
   this includes a shiny duplicate.
5. In every later non-exempt Safari encounter, block Safari Ball use without
   decrementing the player's remaining Safari Balls.
6. Keep the remaining Safari battle actions and flow available, including
   bait, mud, watching, and running.

This gives the Safari Zone one standard encounter opportunity for the whole
save, not one per visit or one per Safari subarea. Non-duplicate shiny
encounters are exempt and do not consume or reset that opportunity. A separate
`safariEncounterUsed` save field is unnecessary.

## Bug-Catching Contest

Contest entry itself does not consume the saved opportunity. The player may
enter repeatedly and use the normal catching, replacement, judging, and prize
flows until a Contest ends with a final selected Pokémon to retain.

After a Contest Pokémon has previously been retained:

- temporary Contest catches remain available so judging and prizes still work;
- the final selected Pokémon is discarded instead of being transferred to the
  party or PC unless it is genuinely shiny;
- a genuinely shiny final selection may be retained under the shiny clause
  without resetting the saved state;
- normal Pokédex updates remain in place, so a species caught during a later
  entry can count as a duplicate elsewhere.

Use one persistent field:

```c
u8 bugContestPokemonKept;
```

At Contest exit, inspect the Contest object's caught flag before the normal
party restoration runs. If no Pokémon will be transferred, leave the saved
field unchanged. If a Pokémon will be transferred and none has previously been
kept, set `bugContestPokemonKept`. If the field is already set, suppress a
non-shiny transfer by clearing the Contest caught flag; the shiny clause still
permits a shiny transfer.

### Later scheduling change

After the capture rules are complete, remove the weekday restriction so the
Bug-Catching Contest can be entered every day. Preserve its other entry
requirements and its existing once-per-day participation limit unless those
rules are changed separately.

## Planned save structure

The capture-rule save block only needs the area bitset and Contest state:

```c
typedef struct CaptureRulesSave {
    u32 consumedAreas[CAPTURE_AREA_WORD_COUNT];
    u8 bugContestPokemonKept;
    u8 padding[3];
} CaptureRulesSave;
```

It does not need an activation flag, a Safari-specific flag, an evolutionary
family bitset, or an area override table.

3. **Complete:** Implement named helpers for reading and updating the

4. **Complete:** Implement the save-level exact-species duplicate check through
   the Pokédex.
5. **Complete:** Classify eligible and excluded encounters where wild
   `BattleSetup` data is created.
6. **Complete:** Apply the ordinary encounter-consumption algorithm.
7. **Complete:** Apply the shiny-clause exemption before ordinary area and
   duplicate enforcement.
8. **Complete:** Block Poké Ball use with clear messages for consumed areas
   and duplicate species.
9. **Complete:** Integrate Safari behavior using its shared displayed
   map-section bit and shiny exception.
10. **Complete:** Record the first Contest Pokémon that is actually retained
    and suppress later final non-shiny transfers.
11. **Complete:** Review all battle exits so temporary encounter and Contest
    state is always cleared.
12. **Complete:** Keep wild double battles disabled.
13. **Build complete; focused manual verification pending:** Verify Safari and
    the revised Contest retention-on-success behavior with an in-game save.

Safari permissions are initialized centrally from the battle type, wild
Pokémon, save data, and the map-section value already copied into
`BattleSystem`. Ball blocking wraps the selection-screen command: the original
handler first records the choice, then the capture rule can return a blocked
choice to the command menu before the later item or Safari throw handler
decrements a Ball.

Contest state is connected at `ScrCmd_BugContestAction`, the command that owns
both Contest allocation and deletion. On later entries, its exit path clears
the Contest object's caught flag only for a non-shiny final selection before
the normal party restoration runs. Catching, replacement, judging, prizes, and
Pokédex updates continue through the original paths.

The automated battle-test suite must only be run when explicitly requested.

## Acceptance checks

- The rules apply before the player receives Poké Balls.
- A valid first encounter consumes its displayed map section even if it flees,
  is defeated, or is not caught.
- A duplicate species does not consume an ordinary map section.
- A consumed ordinary map section blocks later captures.
- A genuinely shiny, non-duplicate encounter may be caught in a consumed area.
- A shiny encounter whose exact species is already caught remains blocked by
  the duplicate rule.
- A non-duplicate shiny encounter does not consume an unused area's standard
  opportunity.
- A Shiny Bait encounter receives the same shiny-clause treatment.
- Every Safari subarea shares one saved opportunity.
- The first Safari encounter without a shiny-clause exemption consumes that
  opportunity even when it is a duplicate.
- Later non-exempt Safari Ball attempts are blocked without consuming a Safari
  Ball.
- Non-duplicate Safari shinies may be caught without consuming or resetting
  the standard Safari opportunity.
- A shiny Safari duplicate follows the standard Safari opportunity flow and
  remains blocked from capture.
- Contest entries that end without retaining a Pokémon do not consume the
  saved Contest opportunity.
- The first Contest that ends with a retained Pokémon permanently awards it
  and records that the opportunity was used.
- Later Contest entries can be judged and award prizes but cannot permanently
  award the caught Pokémon unless it is shiny.
- A later implementation makes the Contest available every day without
  unintentionally removing its other requirements or daily limit.
